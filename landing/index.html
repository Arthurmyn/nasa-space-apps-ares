ARES plans one walk on Mars

ARES brings NASA Mars data into one local view and proposes a candidate route between science stops. Time, climb and the limit for walking back sit next to it, and every number says where it came from. It is our entry in the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map.

  • 1 sitethe boxwork on Mount Sharp, Gale crater
  • Up to 6science stops per walk
  • 4 labelsmeasured, model, assumption or proxy on every number

Fits the clock, not the way back

A stop fits the EVA time budget but lies beyond the limit for walking back to the habitat.

Gentle on one grid, steep on another

A slope looks mild on a 463 m grid and turns steep on a 1 m terrain model.

Layers that do not line up

Misaligned layers create false hazards, or false “safe” corridors.

The question ARES is built around: can the crew walk back from every point of the route? Existing public tools are made for browsing regions. ARES is made for one walk.

From science stops to a checked candidate route

  1. Choose stops

    Place up to six science targets. Each has an objective, its evidence, a confidence level and a rover image with credit.

  2. Combine the layers

    Orbital imagery, terrain model, slope and rover images in one window, with resolution and coverage shown for each.

  3. Get a candidate route

    An A* search on a 1 m grid from the habitat through your stops and back. If no route exists, ARES names the blocking constraint instead of drawing a straight line.

  4. Check the limits

    Each leg gets its 3-D distance, climb, time and a relative effort index, checked against the EVA time budget and the walk-back limit.

Unknown terrain is penalised, never treated as free to cross. Steps 3 and 4 are in progress; they are not in the prototype yet.

Real NASA layers, with their limits in plain view

Each layer shows its mission, instrument, product, resolution and coverage, marked native, coarse or missing. A gap is never treated as safe ground.

The site

The area of interest is the boxwork on Mount Sharp in Gale crater (search frame 137–138° E, 5.5–4.3° S). Curiosity has surface radiation and weather records there, thousands of rover images and detailed orbital imagery. Boxwork ridges are 5–9 m wide and 1–2 m high, so the best path is not a straight line.

  • Coverage check done. A HiRISE terrain model with 1 m pixels covers the boxwork area Curiosity drove through. The working area is still preliminary, about 0.6 by 0.9 km.
  • Science question. Diagenesis along fractures in the sulfate-bearing layers, which record groundwater history.

What we do not claim

We do not claim that ridge and hollow chemistry differ: recent work found no chemical difference. Targets are framed as “study diagenesis along fracture X”.

No real-time weather or radiation. Climate figures are historical.

Every number shows where it came from

Each number carries a value, a unit, a source and one of four labels. These are the starting values behind a route; planners can change them and the route is rebuilt.

SEXTANT (MIT) already did metabolic A* routing and a best path home. We do not present that as new. What ARES adds is data from several Mars missions on one site, a walk-back limited by consumables with a point-of-no-return marker, and a source label on every number.
